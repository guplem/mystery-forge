- **Answer and params.** Keep the planned answer unless it cannot work with the mechanic; then pick a better one and say so in your summary. For a built mechanic, give only the params: the toolkit builds the material, and you place it with `{{ '{{artifact}}' }}` in the document whose front matter has `puzzle: {{ item.id }}`. When the params split the material into parts (such as `key_parts`), place each part once with `{{ '{{artifact:' }}{{ item.id }}{{ '.key1}}' }}` and so on, in another of your documents that players hold at this stage or earlier.
- **What the puzzle reveals.** When a hidden clue names {{ item.id }}, the answer (or what the solved material shows) must reveal exactly that fact.
- **The payoff.** Write `reveal_text`: one to three sentences in the voice of the story that players read when they solve the puzzle (on the companion page, or on a story card at the end of the envelope), such as what the answer means for the case. Never write the answer itself in it: a player on paper may see the card too early. It may add no fact that the accusation needs: the accusation must work from the documents alone.
- **The material shows what to solve and where to look, never how.** No sentence of a document explains the decoding method, the order of operations, or which rule to apply. A signpost (a title, an odd detail, a note in the margin) tells players that there is something to solve. Hints 2 and 3 carry the method. An in-world signpost may point at the extraction step (which letters, which order) when players could not guess it, but never at what the result means.
- **The answer format label** names the format only ("two words", "a 4-digit code"), never the category ("a street name", "an animal"): a category makes the answer guessable.
- **Fair.** Everything needed is in the material that players have at this stage. When `config.json` sets `equipment.printer` to `black_and_white`, no clue depends on a color, and no text names a printed color ("ruled off in red").
- **Clues.** Each `clues` entry quotes, character for character, a sentence of a document that players have at this stage or earlier. Start each new clue id with the puzzle id in lower case (`{{ item.id | lower }}-...`), so that it never collides with another clue.
- **Solution.** Steps that a player could follow, each citing the clues it uses. Name other puzzles by their printed code, never by their id.
- **Hints.** Three hints: 1 = which materials you need, 2 = a nudge toward the method, 3 = the method. Each hint cites every clue that the hint before it cites, and more. No hint contains the answer.
- **Hints and the solution describe the printed page.** The builder decides how a built material looks, and it often differs from the plan and the catalog text. Name only a picture, a mark, an arrow, a symbol, or a position that the page shows.
